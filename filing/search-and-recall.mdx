Recall is the payoff. Everything you capture and file exists so that later, when you or an agent needs it, it comes back. Cortex finds things by meaning, not just by matching words.

## Search understands what you mean

Ask a brain "what did we decide about pricing?" and it does not just hunt for the word "pricing". It understands the intent and returns the pages that are actually about that decision, even if they phrase it differently. This is what makes recall feel like asking a knowledgeable colleague rather than running a keyword search.

You can search a brain yourself from the app. Your connected agents search through their [connection](/connect/how-connections-work), across all the brains you can access at once, and every result says which brain it came from, so wide recall never blurs where anything lives.

## Search from any page

Every page of a brain has a search field in the top bar. Tap it, or press `/` on a keyboard, and type two characters. Pages whose title matches come first, instantly, with their path and type. Under them come hits from inside pages, with the passage that matched and your words in bold. The first row is highlighted: Enter opens it, the arrow keys move, Esc closes. The last row, "All results for ...", takes you to the Search page with the query already filled in, where you can switch mode or read more results. On a phone the results take the whole screen under the bar.

## Answers come back as a section

A search result is not a whole page. Each hit carries the passage that matched, up to about 1,500 characters, together with the page's outline: its headings, in order. It also carries when the page was last written and, for a meeting or other dated page, the day it happened, so an agent can answer "when" and tell an old page from a current one. Most questions are answered from that passage alone. When an agent needs more, it asks for the page section by section, by heading, rather than the whole thing. A very long page (over 20,000 characters) is never handed over whole unless the agent asks for it in so many words; it comes back cut at the opening, with the outline and a note on how to read the rest. This is why the [page size rule](/filing/pages-and-structure#the-right-size-for-a-page) matters: a page that is one topic gives a search hit that is the answer.

## Search the original words

A page is what filing wrote down about a meeting, a conversation or a document. It is shorter than the original, so some of what was said is on no page. Cortex keeps the original text of everything you file, and your agent can search that too.

Ask your agent for what someone actually said, or for a detail the page left out, and it searches the original text as well as the pages. What comes back is a passage of a few speaker turns in the speakers' own words, never a summary, with the item's title and the day it happened. The agent can read on from that point in the original.

| What you filed | Can its original words be searched? |
|---|---|
| Meeting transcripts | Yes |
| Conversations with your agent | Yes |
| Documents you uploaded | Yes |
| Mail | No. A mail body is deleted once it is filed. |
| An item you deleted | No. It stops being searchable at once. |
| An older version of a document | No, once the newer version is filed. |

Three things to know:

- **It is off unless the search asks for it.** An ordinary search reads pages only, exactly as before. Your agent turns it on when a question calls for it, or when you ask it to look in the transcripts.
- **Anyone who can read the brain can search its original text.** In a client brain, a search for one client returns only that client's items.
- **A newly filed item is searchable within the hour.** Items filed before 20 November 2026 are indexed in the background, newest first.

## What makes recall good

The quality of what comes back depends on how well things were filed, which is the whole reason [structure](/filing/pages-and-structure) matters:

- **Citations** mean answers arrive with their sources, so you can trust them and check them.
- **Links** mean that finding one page often surfaces the related ones around it.
- **Clean pages** mean the search is reading tidy write-ups, not raw transcripts, so it finds the substance.

A brain that has been filed well recalls well. That is why Cortex puts effort into filing: it is really an investment in recall.

## Recall through your agent

The most powerful way to use recall is through an agent that is connected to your brain. Instead of searching yourself and reading results, you ask the agent a question and it does the searching, reads the pages, and answers you, with citations. Over time, as your brain fills up, the agent gets more useful, because it has more to draw on.

## Try it

1. Search a brain with a plain-language question rather than keywords.
2. Open a result and follow its citation back to the source.
3. Ask a connected agent the same question and compare how it answers.
