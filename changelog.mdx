<Update label="20 September 2026" description="Your agent chooses which brains to search, and tells you where it looked">
  Until now a question from your agent was searched across every brain you could read, in one go. From today **the agent chooses**. The map it sees when it connects now says what each brain holds, its description, how many pages and its largest folders, and the agent names the brains that could hold the answer, always with your own brain, and keeps a brain that has already answered in the conversation. With no brain named, only your own brain is searched.

  Each brain is now searched **on its own**, at the same time as the others, and the results are merged. A small brain is no longer crowded out by a large one, and no single search ever touches more than one brain.

  Every reply tells the agent which brains were **searched** and which were **not**, with each unsearched brain's description, so when nothing turns up it can widen its search rather than tell you the answer does not exist. Ask it *"which brains did you look in?"* at any time. If you build on the agent tools directly: `query` now answers an object with `hits`, `searched`, `notSearched`, `defaultedTo` and `note`, where it used to answer the list of hits alone, and `list_brains` gains `topFolders`. A brain's description now does real work, so it is worth a line saying what the brain holds and who it is for. See [using multiple brains](/connect/multiple-brains#how-the-agent-chooses).
</Update>

<Update label="19 September 2026" description="Search is quick again on a big brain">
  On a brain of a thousand pages a search from the web app had grown to several seconds. Three changes bring it back under a second, most of which is now the embedding call itself. Cortex runs its search lists **at the same time** rather than one after another. The **passage list**, which looks for your exact words inside long pages, now runs only for a short query of up to three words (an id, a code, part of a word) and in exact mode, because on a whole question it read every passage in the brain and found the pages the other lists had already found. And the **meaning list** uses an index over the embeddings instead of comparing your question with every passage, so it no longer slows down as a brain grows. The web Search page also stops a search you have typed past, on the server too. One thing changes in what comes back: the passage list now finds your words as written inside a page, ignoring case, rather than a near-miss spelling, so an id, a code or part of a word still matches and a typo does not. A long question in hybrid mode gets the same pages, faster.
</Update>

<Update label="17 September 2026" description="Forward mail into any brain, not only your own">
  Until now mail could only be forwarded into the brain marked as yours. From today **any brain can take mail**: a shared brain, a client brain, your own. An Admin gives the brain an address under Settings, then Ingest sources, and the same card now lists the brain's **forwarders**, the addresses mail may come from. Anyone with write access adds an address they can read and confirms it with the six-digit code Cortex sends there. Verification has moved from your profile onto each brain, so the Profile, Mailboxes tab is gone; every address you had verified there is on your own brain's list already, along with the address you sign in with.

  In a **client brain**, forward to the brain's address with `+` and the client's key before the `@` and the thread is filed under that client; the Clients page shows each client's address. Forward to the plain address and the people on the chain decide: one client's domain among them settles it, your own colleagues never count, and a chain with two clients or none waits for someone to choose. The client's own people never have to verify anything; they are the participants, and a client mailing the address directly is refused. In a shared or client brain, Cortex names the member who forwarded a thread in the citation and writes nothing about them; a client's ask lands on the client's page under Open items, never on anyone's task list. The message itself is still never kept.

  Your agent sees each brain's forwarders in `whoami` and `brief`, the health check says when a brain has an address but nobody allowed to send to it, and `cortex mail` manages the list from the command line. See [your email](/capture/your-email#into-a-shared-brain) and [the email contract](/reference/email-contract#forwarders).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Mark your brain as yours from Settings, and mail lives with the other sources">
  Two things moved into the place you would look for them. **Your personal brain** can now be marked from the web app: open a brain's Settings, then General, and if nobody has marked it yet an Owner sees **Make this my personal brain**. That mark is what your agents read to know where to write by default, and it is what lets a brain receive your mail. Before, the command line and the API were the only ways to set it, so a brain made with `cortex init` and never marked could not be given a mail address at all.

  **Forward mail to this brain** has moved from the Connect page to Settings, then **Ingest sources**, beside Granola, Read AI and webhooks, since mail is a source like the others. On your own personal brain it is the same card: create the address, copy it, rotate or remove it, the refused count, and Gmail's confirmation code. On a brain that is not marked as yours, the same spot says why there is no card and points you at General. See [your email](/capture/your-email) and [your personal brain](/brains/your-personal-brain#marked-as-yours).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Search finds more of what a brain holds, in fewer calls">
  Three things made a brain return less than it held, and all three are fixed. A very long page could crowd every other page out of a search, so a brain with one big document looked like one page: each page now gets one place in the running, on its best passage. Exact mode could not find a short string inside a long page (an id, a code, part of a word), because it compared your text with the whole passage rather than looking for it inside: now it looks inside. And a question in plain words only matched pages holding every one of its words: now a page holding most of them counts, most first.

  Two things make each call go further. Ask a **compound question** ("what is our bounce policy and how long does a warm-up take") and Cortex searches its parts as well as the whole, in the same call, so both halves come back together; an agent that knows the parts can pass them as `queries`. And every page now carries a one-line **abstract**, taken from its frontmatter description or its first paragraph, on every search hit, in the page index and in the brief, so an agent can choose the right page without reading it.

  For anyone watching what their agent costs: the usage record now names the tool behind every call, and each search records what it did. See [the API reference](/reference/api#what-a-search-hit-carries).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Cortex on your phone, and a quick add for tasks and topics">
  The Workspace now fits a phone. The page fills the screen, **Pages** slides the folder tree in from the left and **Info** the backlinks and page details from the right; reading, the editor (the same one, both modes), history and delete all work as on a desk. Every brain page also has a round **+** button at the bottom left with two one-line forms: **Task** adds a line to your [task page](/filing/tasks) with a due date and a section, and **Topic** adds something to raise with a person, found by typing their name, under **To Discuss** in their Topics section, dated today and linking the page you were reading. Both append to the page as it is at that moment, so nothing else on it is touched. See [pages and structure](/filing/pages-and-structure#on-your-phone).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Tasks: one page, fed by your meetings and mail">
  Your personal brain now keeps your tasks on **one page**, `todos.md` at its root, one line per task in the shape Obsidian's Tasks plugin uses (due, added, done, priority, recurrence, wikilinks). Cortex fills it: an action item that is yours in a filed meeting, an ask in a forwarded mail, a commitment you made in an agent session, each lands under **Captured** with the date if one was said and links to where it came from, for you to move up. Your agent can list what is due, add a task, tick it or move it, and its morning brief carries the counts. A **Tasks** page in the brain's sidebar shows the list by section with a due strip, tick boxes, quick add and move. Ticked tasks move to an archive page a week later on their own, and a recurring task ticked comes back with its next date. See [tasks](/filing/tasks).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Proposals edit a page, they no longer replace it">
  When Cortex files a meeting or an email on its own and needs to add something to a page that already exists, it now proposes **the change, not the page**: a dated line at the top of a person's timeline, a topic under a heading, one section rewritten. Before, a proposal carried the whole page as Cortex had rewritten it, so anything you or your agent wrote on that page while the proposal waited in the review queue was lost when you accepted it. Now the rest of the page is never touched, an addition applies to the page as it is when you accept, and a rewritten section is refused with nothing written if the page changed underneath it, so you can look again. The review queue shows each change as an addition or a section, with the current page beside it. See [the review queue](/filing/review-queue).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Is your brain still being fed?">
  A brain set up once can quietly stop receiving: the session hook lost with a rebuilt laptop, a meeting tool's webhook switched off, a credential that expired, an inbox nobody cleared. Ask your agent to **run the health check** and it answers in one pass, worst first, with the one thing that fixes each: when the last transcript arrived on the hook, whether each connected source is still delivering, which credentials are unused or expired, how long the oldest inbox item has waited, whether filing is paused, and whether search can see everything. Tell the brain what it should expect under a `## Expectations` heading on its instructions page and the check grades against that instead of the defaults. The same report is `cortex health` on the command line and one address on the API, so a scheduled job can alert on it. See [brain health](/brains/health#is-it-still-being-fed).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Hand a waiting item to your agent, compaction for pages that grow, and a front page for every brain">
  **Deal with the inbox from your agent.** Open a waiting item and the drawer now says whose lane it is in and why it is waiting: Cortex is on it, filing is off for the brain, the monthly limit is reached, or it is simply next. It offers three ways out, one of them a prompt to copy into Claude Code or any connected agent: "Check my Cortex inbox and deal with what is waiting." The agent works through proposals first, then takes pending items from the queue, files them with the matching skill and asks you what Cortex would have parked on. A service credential cannot take items; only you, or an agent acting as you.

  **Pages that grow are compacted, not cut.** A person, company or project page in your own brain that grows past 2,000 words is rewritten in place under 1,500 words, with what is currently true said once, the last six months of dated lines kept as they were, and older lines moved to a history page. Nothing is deleted. Everything else still splits into an overview and topic pages. See [the right size for a page](/filing/pages-and-structure#the-right-size-for-a-page).

  **A front page for every brain.** `_index.md` at the root now lists what is in the brain, folder by folder, kept current by the hourly maintenance until you edit it; then it is yours. See [the front page](/filing/pages-and-structure#the-front-page).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Connect Granola or Read AI and your meetings file themselves">
  **Source connectors.** A brain's Settings page now has a gallery of tools to connect. Pick **Granola**: paste your API key, choose which notes and folders, and Cortex sets up the webhook at Granola, catches up the last week and files every new note as a meeting as Granola finishes it. Pick **Read AI**: paste the address Cortex gives you into Read's webhook settings, and every meeting report arrives with its summary, action items, key questions and transcript. Both are in beta.

  **One source, one brain.** A connected source feeds only the brain you connected it to; nothing routes between brains. On a shared brain, limit a Granola source to a folder, or everything the key can see becomes visible to every reader; the page warns you.

  **Managed at the provider, visible in Cortex.** Every source row shows the last delivery, the last item filed, the last error, what the provider reports about the webhook, and a **Manage** link to the provider's own settings. If a provider switches a webhook off after days of failures, the row says so and a nightly catch-up picks up what was missed. **Deliveries** lists everything that arrived in the last thirty days and what became of it.

  The reference Granola pusher script is retired: connect Granola instead, or ask your agent to file a transcript by hand. See [Granola](/capture/granola), [Read AI](/capture/read-ai) and [meeting transcripts](/capture/meeting-transcripts).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Your email: what mattered goes in, the mail itself never does">
  **Your email.** Mail does two jobs, and Cortex now does the second one. Forward a thread to your brain and it is filed as what mattered: dated lines on the people, companies and projects it touched, todos for the asks, a line in the day's note, every one cited to the message. There is no page per thread and no page that reads like a mailbox.

  **The message is never kept.** A mail item's body exists only while it waits to be filed, and is destroyed the moment filing ends, on archive, on reject, on delete, or on the seventh day unfiled. What remains is the subject, the link back to your mailbox, the date and the pages it was filed to; the Sources record says "Body not kept". Attachments never arrive, only their names. Any caller can ask for the same treatment for anything else with `retain=none`.

  **Two routes in.** Forward a message by hand, or set a rule in your mailbox that forwards the senders you care about, to an address your own brain issues on its Connect page. Or run the reference collector on your side with your own Gmail credential, on a schedule, reading only the threads you selected. Both produce the same thing and go through the same door.

  **One mailbox, one brain.** Mail may only enter the brain marked as yours, and only from an address you have verified under Profile, then Mailboxes, with a six-digit code. A shared brain, a client brain, somebody else's brain: refused, with nothing stored, even when an agent is asked to do it. A refused delivery is counted on the brain with its date and the sender's domain, and never bounced.

  See [your email](/capture/your-email), [the email contract](/reference/email-contract) and [data and privacy](/account/data-and-privacy#mail-is-never-kept).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="The command line is on npm">
  `npm install -g @oncortex/cli` puts `cortex` on your path on any machine with Node 18 or newer. Sign in with `cortex login`, then analyse, import and verify a folder of notes, upload files as sources, mark your brain as yours, or empty a test brain. Agents that find it installed stream files through it instead of asking for a one-time upload address. See [the command line](/connect/command-line).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="What's new: a command line, a connector for organisations, and agents that file what they upload">
  The changes of the past few days add up to one shift: **the door decides who files an item**, and the tools around that door got better.

  **A command line.** `cortex` signs in as you, the way Claude Code does, and does the jobs a browser is bad at: `analyse` a folder of markdown notes, `init` a brain, `import` the folder into it and `verify` the result from the brain's side; `upload` a file straight from disk; `personal` to mark a brain as yours; `empty` a brain to run something again from scratch. It paces itself under the API's rate limit and a stopped import is safe to run again. Install it with `npm install -g @oncortex/cli`. See [the command line](/connect/command-line).

  **Claude for your whole organisation.** A Claude admin can add Cortex once as an organisation connector: Claude on the web registers itself with Cortex's sign-in service and keeps a secret Cortex issues it once, each member signs in through the browser, and Claude reaches exactly the brains that member can. Before this, hosted clients that keep a secret were turned away at registration. The connector now shows the Cortex icon in the list. See [connecting Claude](/connect/claude).

  **Agents file what they upload.** What a connected agent brings in, a document you handed it, a transcript, a note, the agent files itself, in the same conversation, with its own tokens; the item shows as held by your agent and Cortex's own filer never touches it. What arrives with nobody present (a web upload, a webhook, a session hook, a file sent from the command line) is filed by Cortex. An item an agent starts and abandons goes to the review queue after 30 minutes. Inbox items can now also be **deleted** by an admin, original and all, as well as archived. See [the inbox](/capture/the-inbox).

  **One-time upload tokens, and the text handed back.** An agent with a shell no longer passes a file's bytes through the conversation. It asks Cortex for a token good for one brain, one file, one use and 15 minutes, sends the file from its shell, and gets back the inbox item, its citable address and the document's text, extracted on the server, plus what changed when the file is a new version of a document already filed. Then it writes the pages and archives the item. With the command line installed, the upload streams from disk instead. See [ask your agent](/capture/asking-your-agent#giving-your-agent-a-file) and the [API reference](/reference/api#upload-a-file-with-a-one-time-token).

  **A monthly ingestion limit, in place of the daily allowance.** Cortex's own filing is now paid for by the workspace under a monthly spend limit. When the month's total reaches it, automatic filing pauses for the workspace, the inbox says so with the spend and the limit, your agent is told when it connects, and items wait until the month turns or the limit is raised. What your agent uploads is unaffected. The per-brain daily allowance is gone. See [how ingestion is paid for](/filing/how-ingestion-is-paid-for).

  **Section-sized answers, and a size rule for pages.** A search hit now carries the passage that matched, up to about 1,500 characters, and the page's outline, so most questions are answered from the hit. Agents and the API read a page by section, by heading, and a page over 20,000 characters is never handed over whole unless asked for. The rule for a page is one topic, 300 to 1,500 words; the Health page counts pages over 2,000 words and lists them, and Cortex queues each for the new **split page** skill, which rewrites it as a short overview at the same address plus one page per topic, through the review queue. See [search and recall](/filing/search-and-recall) and [pages and structure](/filing/pages-and-structure).

  **The first-document check.** Before its first write into a brain, an agent checks the brain's instructions. If they are thin (nothing written, the untouched starter text, under sixty words, or silent on folders that hold pages), it asks you what the brain is for, or shows a short draft, and saves the instructions only after you reply. See [ask your agent](/capture/asking-your-agent#the-first-time-an-agent-writes-into-a-brain).

  **Your brain is the default, and it is marked as yours.** One brain per person carries a mark that says "this is my personal brain", and that is what an agent reads: it writes there unless you name another brain, confirms the first time it writes to a shared brain in a conversation, and asks if nothing is marked. The brain Cortex created for you is marked already; one you created yourself is marked from the command line or the API (no Settings switch yet). See [your work brain](/brains/your-personal-brain#marked-as-yours).

  **Brains grow with use.** A page saved to a new folder creates that folder, and your agent sees every folder that holds pages, described in Settings or not, beside the intended structure, so it files into the folders you have rather than inventing new ones. The Personal template gained homes for companies and meetings. Describe a new folder in Settings once it settles. And a deleted brain keeps its address, so creating a brain with a deleted brain's slug now says so instead of failing. See [pages and structure](/filing/pages-and-structure#brains-grow-with-use).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="An editor that reads like the page, and pictures on pages">
  Editing a page now **looks like reading it**. Headings, bold, lists, quotes, code and links render as you type, and the markdown marks show only on the line your caret is on, so the text stays plain markdown underneath and an untouched page saves nothing. Wikilinks show as links you can click, task boxes tick with a click, and `[[` still completes page names. A **Source** switch shows the raw markdown when you want it. And you can **paste or drop an image** into a page: it is kept as a source of the brain, with the page recorded as where it was used, and only the brain's own images render as pictures. Agents can look at a page's images through their connection.
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="The Workspace first, folder and bulk deletes, and fullscreen">
  Opening a brain now goes **straight to the Workspace**: the tree, the page and the backlinks. The separate landing page with a flat list of pages is gone; the tree is that list. In the tree, hover a folder to **delete it** with everything under it, or press **Select** to tick pages and folders and delete them together. Each confirmation lists what goes, asks you to type a word above ten pages, and reports anything it could not delete and why; version history is kept as ever. And the Workspace has a **fullscreen** button (or Ctrl or Cmd+Shift+F): the app chrome goes, the editor grows to the window, Esc brings it back, and the choice is remembered.
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Manage a brain's members from the API">
  Onboarding can now be **scripted**. The public API lists a brain's members and grants, changes or revokes access by email or user id, with the same rules as the Members page: Admin or Owner on the brain to manage it, workspace members only, one grant per person, and the last Owner stays. A service credential counts with the role it was issued, so a read-and-write credential cannot widen access. This is deliberately not something an agent can do from inside a conversation. See the [API reference](/reference/api#members).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Ingest sources on the Settings page">
  Webhook sources now have a **screen**: Settings > Ingest sources on any brain you administer. Create a source with its label, authentication mode, guard rules, target folder, client pin and default filing skill, and copy the secret, the delivery URL, the headers and a ready-made `curl` example from the one-time reveal panel. Edit a source's rules and hints later without re-issuing the secret, and delete one by typing its label; what it already delivered stays put. The block is not the Sources archive in the side navigation, and says so. See [receiving webhooks](/reference/webhooks).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Name the filing skill and skip the triage check">
  Every way into the inbox can now **say what the item is**: a "File as" choice on the web upload, a `skill` field on the API and your agent's upload tool, an `X-Ingest-Skill` header on webhooks, and a standing default on a webhook source. When you name the skill, the automatic filer skips its "is this worth filing" check for that item, spends nothing on it, and files with the skill you chose; the item's log says so. Leave it unset and ambient captures are checked exactly as before. A name that is not a skill is refused with the list. See [uploading files](/capture/uploading-files), the [API reference](/reference/api) and [webhooks](/reference/webhooks).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Adding to a page, what changed, the graph tools, moves, and one search tool">
  Agents and integrations can now **add to one part of a page** instead of replacing it: a timeline line at the top of a section, a topic or todo under a heading, a note at the end, or a frontmatter key, with everything else on the page left exactly as it was. If the page changed under them, the edit is refused and they retry. A **changes feed** tells an agent what was created, updated or deleted since it last looked, with the inbox counts, so a morning briefing reads only what is new. Four **graph and timeline tools** join the connection: who links to a page, the pages within a few hops of it, a page's timeline as dated entries, and a check that the names an agent is about to link actually exist (with near misses when they do not), plus a one-call way to add a dated timeline line. **Moving or renaming a page** now keeps the graph whole: every page that linked to the old name is rewritten, and a move never overwrites. And search is **one tool with a mode**: exact for names, ids and phrases, semantic for meaning, hybrid for both; the separate exact-search tool is retired. The operating manual (1.7.0) explains all of it. See the [API reference](/reference/api).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Service credentials in the audit trail, and connection lifetimes">
  A page written with a **service credential** now shows up in the brain's audit trail as that credential, by its id and label, instead of with no author. Nothing changes for the credential itself. And **how long a connection lasts** is now a stated rule rather than a library default: an agent stays connected while it is used at least once every 90 days, and is asked to approve again if it sits idle longer. See [how connections work](/connect/how-connections-work).
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Where a document lives, and revisions">
  Every inbox item can now say **where its document lives**: a link to the original on SharePoint, OneDrive, Google Drive, Confluence or anywhere on the web, with the provider, its id and the version at the time. The source record shows the copy that was read beside a link to the live document, and page citations carry both. Your agent can send the **file itself** over its connection rather than a text conversion, so the archived original is the document. And when a newer version of a known document arrives it is kept as a **revision**: linked to the earlier item, which is marked superseded, with a line diff of what changed handed to whoever files it. Nothing is filed automatically because a document changed; the brain's filing mode applies as for any new item.
</Update>

<Update label="September 2026" description="Partitions: one brain, many clients">
  A brain can now be **partitioned**: one folder per client (or site, or matter) with a hub page, email domains and other names, managed on a new Clients page with CSV import. Nothing files under a client that does not exist, and every arriving item is matched to its client from the sender's address, the attendee emails or the title, or waits as "needs a client" with one-click assignment that remembers the domain. Search, the page index and your agent's tools narrow to one client. Archive keeps a finished client readable; purge, for owners and only after archiving, removes it with every version. The Client template ships partitioned, the API covers all of it, and a reference Granola pusher shows the transcript route end to end.
</Update>

<Update label="July 2026" description="Review proposals, manual filing and the transcript format">
  The review queue grew up. Parked items now carry a full **proposal**: the drafted pages themselves, editable and acceptable in one click from the web, from your agent, or automatically at high confidence. You can also file any inbox item entirely by hand, no agent needed. Waiting items surface as badges in the app, as counts your agent sees, and as a morning email digest once they have waited a day.

  Meeting capture moved to a push-only **transcript format**: any tool that can POST a file can feed a brain, with attendee emails driving people-matching and safe re-delivery built in. The Granola polling source is retired; ingest URLs now accept a folder hint (`?path=`).
</Update>
